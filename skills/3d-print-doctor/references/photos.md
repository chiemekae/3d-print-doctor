# Asking for photos

The printer's camera sees one fixed angle in poor light. Most of what decides "worked" or "failed" (the underside, the fit, a close-up of a defect) only shows in photos the user takes with their phone. Ask for the specific shots below rather than "send a picture". Ask once, as a short numbered list the user can follow at the printer, and say why each shot matters in a few words.

## How to take them

Put these tips in the request, adapted to the part:

- **Light from a low side angle.** A phone flashlight or a lamp held off to one side, almost level with the surface, makes layer lines, strings, and gaps cast shadows. Overhead light and the phone's flash flatten everything, and dark filament hides almost all detail under them.
- **One wide shot, then close-ups.** The wide shot shows where on the part each close-up is. Fill the frame with the defect in each close-up, and hold the phone steady or tap to focus.
- **Something for scale** in any shot about size or fit: a ruler, a coin, or calipers on the feature.
- **Same angle as before** when comparing a test print with the original failure, so the difference is the print and not the photo.
- **Plain background** when you can. A cluttered desk competes with the part.

## Print just finished, still on the plate

Ask for these when a watch reports `finished`, and before the user removes the part. They're the only chance to see how the part sat on the plate.

1. **Wide shot of the whole part on the plate**, from above at an angle.
2. **Low, side-on shot of the base where it meets the plate**, along a long edge and at one corner: shows lifting or warping and how the first layer bonded.
3. **Close-up of anything that looks off**: strings, blobs, gaps, a rough patch.

The user can remove the part once you've seen these.

## After removal

4. **The underside**: the first layer's finish shows squish, gaps, and plate contact.
5. **Close-ups of each defect**, with low side lighting.
6. **The part doing its job**: fitted, mounted, or holding what it's for. This is what decides success for a functional part. Ask how it fits (too tight, loose, rocks) in a sentence too.

## Shots by failure

When diagnosing, ask for the shots that separate the suspects for that failure (headings match [failure-modes.md](failure-modes.md)):

| Failure | Ask for |
|---|---|
| Stringing, blobs, zits | Wide shot with the light behind or beside the strings (they show up against a dark background); a close-up of the worst area; the seam line. |
| First layer, adhesion, warping | The underside, flat and in raking light; a side-on shot of a lifted corner with something straight (a ruler) along the base; the plate surface where the part sat. |
| Elephant foot, dimensions, fit | Side-on view of the bottom edge; calipers or a ruler on the measured feature, with the reading visible; the mating part beside it. |
| Under- or over-extrusion, wall gaps | Close-up of the top surface and of a wall, in raking light; a thin wall held up to a light, which shows gaps. |
| Layer shift, ringing, Z banding | Side-on shot of the whole height so the shift or bands line up; a close-up near the corner or feature where it starts. |
| Overhangs, bridges | From below, looking up at the overhang or bridge. |
| Supports | The supported surface after removal; any support left stuck on. |
| Spaghetti, detached mid-print | The plate as it is, before clearing it; the nozzle and hotend from the front, for a blob of filament. |
| Clog, nothing extruding | The nozzle tip from below with a light, and the extruder gear area if the user can reach it. |

If a camera snapshot or the job's timelapse already shows something (where on the plate, at what layer), say so and ask only for what the camera can't see.
